✂️ Task 1 — Cutting & Editing Objects 45 min

Learn to remove or cut objects cleanly using three essential Photoshop tools. Each tool suits different situations — choose based on complexity.

Document setup: File → New → A3 Landscape · 420×297mm · 300 dpi · CMYK

The Three Tools

Object Selection Tool (W)

Best for: whole objects on clear backgrounds

  • Hover to preview blue highlight
  • Click or drag a box around the object
  • Press Delete → Content-Aware Fill
  • Shift/Option to add or subtract areas

Remove Tool (J)

Best for: small marks, blemishes, distractions

  • Choose from Contextual Task Bar or Shift+J
  • Brush over unwanted elements
  • AI fills the space seamlessly

Pen Tool (P)

Best for: precise or complex edges

  • Click for straight points
  • Click and drag for curves
  • Hold Alt/Opt to change curve direction
  • Right-click → Make Selection

Step-by-Step — click each step to expand

1
Prepare Your Document
▼
  • File → New → A3 Landscape · 420×297mm · 300 dpi · CMYK
  • Add white background: Edit → Fill → White
  • Place your image: File → Place Embedded…
2
Object Selection Tool — Quick Removal
▼
  • Select Object Selection Tool (W) from toolbar
  • Hover over your object — blue highlight previews the selection
  • Click or drag to select
  • Press Delete to activate Content-Aware Fill
Tip: Use Shift+click to add areas to your selection, Option+click to remove.
3
Remove Tool — Brush Away Small Elements
▼
  • Select Remove Tool (J) from the Contextual Task Bar or use Shift+J to cycle
  • Adjust brush size to suit the element
  • Paint over logos, wires, dust, or unwanted objects
  • Photoshop AI fills the background area automatically
4
Pen Tool — Precise Cutouts
▼
  • Select Pen Tool (P) from the toolbar group
  • Click to place anchor points around your object
  • Click and drag to create smooth curves
  • Hold Alt/Option while dragging to change curve direction
  • Complete the path by clicking the start point
  • Right-click → Make Selection → use Delete or refine further
5
Save Your Work
▼
  • Save as .PSD — keeps all layers editable
  • Export as .JPG — for Padlet upload
  • Take a screenshot of your progress for the Progress Report

Font Guidance

✅ Recommended Fonts

These work well for research pages — clean, editorial, structured.

Andale Mono
Orator Std
Typewriter Condensed
OldNewspaperTypes — subheadings & titles

You can also download fonts from dafont.com or fonts.google.com — on your own computer only due to admin restrictions.

🚫 Banned Fonts — Do Not Use

These are not appropriate for academic design work.

Chalkboard
Bradley Hand
Chalkduster
Why? These fonts undermine the visual quality and editorial feel of your research page. They read as informal and unprofessional in this context.

Step-by-Step — click each step to expand

1
Select Your Keywords
▼
  • Choose a handful of words or short phrases from your artist research
  • Open a new Photoshop document — A3 landscape · 300 dpi
  • Use Type Tool (T) to type out your words
  • Try minimal, structured fonts: Andale Mono · Orator Std · Typewriter Condensed
Why keywords? Short phrases isolate ideas and emotions — easier to explore visually than long blocks of text.
2
Rasterize and Duplicate
▼
  • In the Layers panel: right-click → Rasterize Type
  • Duplicate the rasterized layer: Cmd/Ctrl+J — keeps a backup
  • Work on the duplicate, not the original
3
Create Glitches
▼
  • Use Rectangular Marquee Tool (M) to select thin horizontal strips of text
  • Press Cmd/Ctrl+J to copy each strip to a new layer
  • Use Move Tool (V) to shift strips slightly left or right
  • Subtle displacement creates a glitch effect
4
Wave Distortion
▼
  • Select your strips and Merge Layers: Cmd/Ctrl+E
  • Go to Filter → Distort → Wave for controlled ripple effects
  • Adjust wavelength, amplitude, and number of generators
  • Optionally add Filter → Noise → Add Noise for texture
5
Integrate with Your Layout
▼
  • Position the glitch text within your A3 composition
  • Use as a background element or a feature component
  • Align with your artist study or research theme
  • Keep the outcome meaningful and reflective
6
Save and Screenshot
▼
  • Save as .PSD — all layers stay editable
  • Export as .JPG for Padlet upload
  • Take 1–2 screenshots of your process for the Progress Report

📝 Task 3 — Document & Reflect 30 min

Complete your Progress Report — documenting your process and reflecting on what you learned. This is an assessed skill at Level 5.

Why reflect? Documentation shows how you learned, not just what you produced. Reflective writing demonstrates critical thinking — a key Level 5 outcome.

How to Complete Your Progress Report

1

2
Enter Your Name and Upload Screenshots
▼

  • Add a short caption to each screenshot describing what it shows
3

▼

  • 2 — Getting there

4

▼

2. Challenges & solutions

3. What worked well

Which part of your outcome are you most satisfied with?

4. Next time

What would you do differently? What will you explore further?

  • Be specific — name actual tools and decisions you made
  • Use evaluative language: "this worked because…", "this was difficult because…"
  • Aim for 3–5 sentences per question

  • Your PDF will include all screenshots, captions, and reflections
  • Save the file somewhere you can easily find it
  • You will upload this to Padlet in the Submit step
